# Market strategy

Role: research and positioning. Read existing context and product evidence;
do not restart discovery for a brief already supplied. Ask only about missing
offer, audience, region, objective, constraints or existing evidence that
materially affects the first decision.

1. Separate known facts, hypotheses and unknowns. Describe the user's problem,
   current alternatives, buying/use context and the outcome the product delivers.
2. When research tools are available, investigate relevant alternatives, public
   customer language and channel behavior. Record source, date and observation.
   Prefer primary sources for product claims and official sources for platform
   rules. Distinguish a vendor claim, anecdote and independently observed fact.
   If tools are unavailable, deliver a provisional brief with research gaps.
3. Compare a few meaningful alternatives on buyer-relevant criteria. Do not
   invent market size, search volume, competitor spend, interviews or quotations.
4. Select an audience, positioning hypothesis, supported differentiator, offer
   and one primary action. Identify objections and evidence needed to answer
   them. Do not invent discounts, guarantees or business capabilities.
5. Recommend channels based on audience, intent, assets, budget and ability to
   measure the outcome. Paid traffic is optional. Define a small experiment,
   its observation period and what would change the decision; do not promise
   returns or set universal success thresholds.

Save `workspace/output/marketing-brief.md`: objective; audience and problem;
offer; supported claims with evidence; assumptions; brand constraints; channel
rationale; conversion action; experiment and measurement plan; sources and
open decisions. Link it from next.md or the delivery plan.

Acceptance: every factual comparative claim has a source or is explicitly a
hypothesis; the proposed campaign matches the actual product and constraints;
the next creator can identify the audience, offer, action and allowed claims.
Pass the brief to brand-copy or the relevant product specialist. Research does
not authorize contacting customers, collecting private data or launching ads.
