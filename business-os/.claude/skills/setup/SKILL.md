# Business setup

The first interaction should feel like a conversation, not a form. Ask one
question at a time and wait for the answer. Aim for five minutes and no more
than eight questions.

## Reuse what is already known

Before asking, check whether the answer is in the conversation. Propose a
reasonable interpretation when possible and let the owner correct it. Use open
questions for facts that cannot be inferred, such as the name, a real writing
sample or the week's priority.

## Interview

1. The business name and what it does in one sentence.
2. Who it serves: a brief description of a typical customer.
3. A real piece of the owner's writing: a post, email or customer reply.
   Derive voice from this example rather than asking an abstract tone question.
4. The actual priority for the next two weeks.
5. Existing colors, fonts and logo, if available. Otherwise skip this.
6. Relevant categories now: content, advertising or operations. Do not activate
   categories the owner will not use in the coming weeks.

For a vague answer, ask for something concrete once. If it remains vague,
record what is known and continue rather than blocking setup.

## Save the context

- `memory/company.md`: answers 1–2.
- `memory/preferences.md`: derive two or three sentences of voice from answer 3
  and identify the source example.
- `memory/strategy.md`: answer 4, dated today.
- `identity/design-guide.md`: answer 5 when available.
- Activate only available skills in the categories selected in answer 6.
  Unavailable categories remain documented as inactive, not partly installed.

Keep memory inside the 60/60/40-line budgets in CLAUDE.md. Summarize before
writing if the interview supplies more than fits.

## First useful action

Finish with a four- or five-line understanding of the business and a concrete
first task based on the current priority. Make the suggestion specific enough
to produce something useful with real input, rather than promising future work.
